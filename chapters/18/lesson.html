Can unlabeled readings reveal operating regimes?

A failure classifier needs labels. An operations team may instead ask which sensor patterns recur and which readings look unusual before labels arrive. Clustering groups observations by a chosen geometry; it does not discover failure classes or prove that the groups are natural kinds.

For n vectors x and k centers μ, the k-means objective is Σi min_j ||x_i−μ_j||². The outer sum visits observations, and the minimum selects a center. Lloyd's algorithm alternates closest-center assignments with within-cluster means. Each exact step cannot increase this objective, but initialization can lead to different local solutions.

What if membership is uncertain?

A reading between two operating regimes need not receive a hard identity. A Gaussian mixture combines k density components: p(x)=Σj πj N(x;μj,Σj), where nonnegative weights π sum to 1, μ is a mean, and Σ describes spread. This lab uses diagonal covariance: each component stores one variance per coordinate and assumes coordinate independence within that component.

Two one-dimensional components with equal weights, means 0 and 2, and variance 1 assign equal responsibility to query 1. At query 0, their relative density masses are 1 and exp(−2)≈.1353 because the common Gaussian factor cancels. Normalizing gives responsibilities about.8808 and.1192.

A responsibility is a posterior component-membership fraction given the current mixture. It is not a probability of machine failure. Unlike k-means' binary assignments, every row can contribute fractional mass to every component. Mixture components can have unequal weights and spreads; a single diagonal component still cannot represent an arbitrarily rotated elliptical covariance.

The supplied Gmm::log_terms adds the log weight to each component's Gaussian log density. responsibilities subtracts the maximum log term, exponentiates and normalizes the resulting masses. For log terms[−1000,−1002], use[1, exp(−2)] rather than underflowing both exponentials to zero.

fn squared_distance(features: Point, other_features: Point) -> f64 {
    features
        .into_iter()
        .zip(other_features)
        .map(|(value, other_value)| (value - other_value).powi(2))
        .sum()
}

Build the E-step from a frozen mixture

Expectation-maximization (EM) alternates two computations. The E-step calculates each row's responsibility under the current mixture. The M-step fits new parameters from those fractional assignments. We cannot update the first component and then use it to calculate the second component's responsibilities: that mixes two model states.

Fit means, variances and weights from fractional counts

For readings[0,2] and first-component responsibilities[.75,.25], its effective count is 1. Its new mean is(.75×0+.25×2)/1=.5. Its variance is(.75×.5²+.25×1.5²)/1=.75. With two total observations its new mixture weight is 1/2. Fractional counts replace integer cluster counts.

In general N_j=Σi r_ij, μ_j=Σi r_ij x_i/N_j, variance_jd=Σi r_ij(x_id−μ_jd)²/N_j, and π_j=N_j/n. The variance uses the new mean and the same frozen responsibilities. Apply max(variance, floor) to every coordinate. A Gaussian can otherwise collapse onto a row with almost zero variance and unbounded density.

How unusual is this observation under the fitted density?

An anomaly score can be the negative log density, −ln p(x). For densities.2 and.01, scores are about 1.609 and 4.605: lower density produces a higher score. Density is not probability at an exact continuous point; it depends on feature units, and a density above 1 produces a negative score without violating probability laws.

The report ranks the eighteen development validation readings by negative log density and prints their validation indices. It also prints the first row's component responsibilities. The most unusual observation is not necessarily a failure: it may be a healthy new operating regime, a changed measurement unit, or a failed sensor.

Projection can hide anomalies. A row differing only along PCA's discarded third direction may have an ordinary two-dimensional score. Train-only fitting prevents leakage but does not make this geometric choice suitable for every operational question.
