What does a generator owe six observations?

The training values are [−1.4,−1.0,−0.7,0.7,1.0,1.4]. Their mean is 0, but a model that always emits 0 misses both visible clusters. A generator needs a distribution of outputs, not merely the best single average prediction.

This chapter compares three ways to train that distribution: a variational autoencoder connects observations to latent distributions; a GAN learns through a discriminator; a diffusion predictor learns injected noise and supports a reverse sampling procedure. Their losses measure different quantities, so putting their raw numbers in a winner column would be meaningless.

All observations, latent values, and predictions here are scalars. The VAE has five parameters, the GAN has two generator and three discriminator parameters, and the diffusion predictor has three. Numerical finite differences keep the optimization visible at that scale. They are an expensive approximation to derivatives, not hidden automatic differentiation and not a sensible method for a large image model.

How does an encoder output a distribution rather than a code?

The encoder computes mean μ=ax+b and one shared log variance ℓ. Its variance is exp⁡(ℓ), standard deviation exp⁡(ℓ2), and latent sample is

z=μ+exp⁡(ℓ2)ε

with ε drawn from a standard normal. For μ=0.8, variance 0.25 and ε=−0.5, z=0.55. Using exp⁡(ℓ) as the standard deviation would give the wrong scale.

This reparameterization separates externally sampled noise from differentiable parameters. Hold ε fixed when estimating a derivative; resampling plus and minus finite-difference evaluations adds random variation to their difference. The lab averages a small fixed symmetric set of noise values for each data point. That is a deterministic teaching approximation to the latent expectation, not an exact normal integral.

The decoder mean is cz+d. With a fixed unit-variance Gaussian observation model, its reconstruction term is half squared error, omitting a parameter-independent constant. The approximate posterior’s KL divergence from a unit Gaussian is

0.5(μ2+exp⁡(ℓ)−1−ℓ).

At μ=0, ℓ=0 the divergence is 0; at μ=1, ℓ=0 it is 0.5.

The minimized objective averages reconstruction plus 0.1×KL. The coefficient makes this a beta-VAE surrogate with β=0.1; it is not the unweighted negative evidence lower bound. At β=1 the familiar ELBO interpretation applies with the declared likelihood and exact expectation. A smaller β trades weaker prior matching for reconstruction pressure.

Reconstructing an observed x uses its encoded posterior. Generating a new decoder mean draws z from the prior, without running the encoder. A full observation from the fixed-variance decoder additionally draws observation noise. Those two sample summaries answer different questions.

Which model is being optimized in this step?

The generator maps noise z to G⁡(z)=az+b. The discriminator forms a logit s⁡(x)=ux2+vx+c and probability D⁡(x)=sigmoid⁡(s⁡(x)). The quadratic term lets it respond to spread as well as location; the affine generator remains a restricted distribution family.

The discriminator minimizes

−mean⁡(log⁡D⁡(real))−mean⁡(log⁡(1−D⁡(fake))).

During this update the generator is fixed. The non-saturating generator minimizes

−mean⁡(log⁡D⁡(G⁡(z)))

while discriminator parameters are fixed. Gradients still pass through the discriminator’s response to its input; freezing its parameters does not mean treating its output as a constant.

At D=0.5 everywhere, each negative log term is ln⁡2. Discriminator loss is 2ln⁡2=ln⁡4≈1.3863, while non-saturating generator loss is ln⁡2≈0.6931. The original minimax generator objective mean⁡(log⁡(1−D⁡(fake))) instead equals −ln⁡2. A sign check at this simple point catches mixing the two objectives.

For a fake logit far below 0, minimax gradients can be weak. The non-saturating objective gives a stronger early push toward larger discriminator scores. Use stable log-sigmoid calculations: log⁡(1−sigmoid⁡(s))=log⁡(sigmoid⁡(−s)); the Rust helper log_sigmoid takes argument (−s). Computing sigmoid first and then log can round to log⁡(0) for extreme logits.

One alternating iteration updates the discriminator using the current generator, then updates the generator using the newly updated discriminator. Both parameter blocks should change on the fixture. A frozen discriminator is a different training procedure even if the generator loss decreases.

Can low loss certify that both modes were learned?

A generator can neglect a region of the data while fooling its present discriminator. That is mode collapse. Compare generated values with the observations through sample spread, quantiles, and coverage of both sides of 0. A single mean cannot distinguish a symmetric broad distribution, two separated clusters, and a point mass at 0.

There is a more basic limitation here: an affine function of Gaussian noise is Gaussian. It cannot exactly represent two separated modes. Neither this affine GAN generator, the linear VAE decoder with Gaussian noise, nor the affine deterministic diffusion chain can reproduce the target’s two-mode shape. Failure of coverage may reflect representation limits before it reflects an optimizer bug.

The program reports 512-sample summaries using deterministic pseudo-random seeds, including VAE decoder means separately from full observations. Finite values and nonzero spread establish that sampling executed; they do not establish distributional agreement. Repeated seeds would quantify variability but would not remove the architectural limitation.

GAN losses can oscillate because each model changes the other’s task. Do not stop at the lowest discriminator loss: a very successful discriminator may expose a poor generator. Keep a fixed sample-noise set for comparisons, state the target distribution, and inspect model capacity before tuning training duration.

How do you manufacture a target for denoising?

Choose cumulative retained-signal fraction α¯t from [0.95,0.6,0.15,0.02]. Generate a noisy observation directly:

xt=α¯tx0+1−α¯tε.

At α¯=0.6, x0=0.7 and ε=−0.4, xt≈0.2892. You know the injected ε because you sampled it; no human denoising label is required.

The predictor receives xt and a timestep feature and outputs ε^. Its objective averages (ε^−ε)2 across observations, fixed noise values, and schedule positions. Timestep conditioning matters because the relationship between signal and noise changes over the schedule. Omitting it asks one function to solve ambiguous tasks.

Sampling starts from noise and visits schedule positions in reverse order. Estimate clean data by

x^0=xt−1−α¯tε^α¯t.

Then form the previous deterministic state as

xt−1=α¯t−1x^0+1−α¯t−1ε^.

At the final transition use previous α¯=1, yielding x^0. Reusing ε^ in this formula defines the deterministic DDIM-style path taught here.

This is not a stochastic DDPM sampler: it adds no fresh reverse-step noise. Also the last forward α¯ is 0.02, rather than exactly 0, so initializing with pure Gaussian noise is an approximation to the forward terminal distribution. Four scalar steps teach accounting, not large-model sampling fidelity.

Which check can isolate the sampler from a weak predictor?

Give the sampler perfect known noise before testing learned predictions. For x0=0.7 and ε=−0.4, construct xt at each α¯. Passing that exact ε to one reverse transition should reconstruct the corresponding previous state exactly within floating-point tolerance. At the last transition it should recover 0.7.

This oracle separates two possible failures: a correct reverse formula with an inaccurate predictor, and a good predictor used by an incorrect formula. The working baseline returns xt unchanged, so it fails even with perfect noise. More training cannot fix that missing transition.

Schedule validation requires a nonempty sequence of finite values strictly between 0 and 1 in decreasing order. Negative values break square roots; a zero current α¯ breaks division; reversing an already reversed schedule applies the wrong process. Name the index convention in both code and reasoning.

Training noise MSE and generated-sample quality remain separate. Error at one reverse step affects later inputs, and a low average denoising loss does not guarantee both target modes. The solution verifies learned noise improvement and exact oracle transitions before presenting sample summaries.
