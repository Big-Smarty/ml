Can each class be represented by a distribution?

Retrieve what a probability density means: a Gaussian assigns probability to intervals through area, not a probability to an exact continuous temperature. A narrow density can exceed 1 while its total area remains 1. For class mean 2, variance 1 and query 3, the Gaussian log-density contribution is−½[ln(2π)+1]≈−1.419.

Bayes' rule combines the prior chance of failure with the likelihood of the observed sensors within each class. In proportional form, posterior(c|x) ∝ prior(c)×likelihood(x|c). Gaussian naive Bayes assumes the coordinates are conditionally independent given the class, then multiplies one Gaussian density per feature. The mean and variance depend on both class and feature.

Multiplying many small densities underflows. Instead add logarithms: log score(c)=ln prior(c)−½Σj[ln(2π)+ln variance[c, j]+(x[j]−mean[c, j])²/variance[c, j]]. The sum visits every feature. Normalize the two class log scores by subtracting their maximum, exponentiating the differences, and dividing by their sum. Unlike the preserved miniature reference, the new lab exposes this normalized binary probability for Brier/calibration comparisons.

The sensor table violates conditional independence: temperature and vibration share latent wear. We also apply the Gaussian approximation to indicator/one-hot coordinates for one transparent common-width comparison; binary columns are not truly Gaussian. A mixed Gaussian/categorical model would be a different assumption. Cheap classification can still work while probability confidence is poor.

fn squared_distance(features: [f64; 2], other_features: [f64; 2]) -> f64 {
    features
        .into_iter()
        .zip(other_features)
        .map(|(value, other_value)| (value - other_value).powi(2))
        .sum()
}
