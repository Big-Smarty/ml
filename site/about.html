About the course

Understand what you build.

First Principles is a practical machine-learning course in Rust, from one scalar neuron to advanced language-model systems.

What “from scratch” means here

You implement the mathematical operations, losses, gradients, optimizers and models yourself. The opening projects use Rust’s standard library. Later chapters introduce GPU access through wgpu, while keeping the model operations in shaders you write. A final chapter connects your understanding to established tools.

How the material was developed

Six Luna High research assignments investigated tutorial formats, learning design, ML foundations, CPU systems, GPU systems and language models. The lead authored Chapter 1 and the shared course design. Sol High authors produced the initial drafts. Astra High authors then took over the last fifteen chapters and review of every chapter, using Luna High or Max researchers for primary-source evidence. Reference programs and numerical checks accompany the explanations; validation limitations are recorded instead of hidden.

The structure draws inspiration from Rustlings, Ziglings, Haskellings, Vulkan Guide, Vulkan Tutorial and LearnOpenGL. These projects are not affiliated with or endorsing this course. The original Vulkan Tutorial now warns that its API guidance is outdated; current GPU references are used for implementation.

Syntax highlighting uses a locally bundled PrismJS 1.30.0 subset under its MIT license. It colors code without changing the text you copy and needs no internet connection.

A note on evidence

Small deterministic checks establish particular properties of a program. They do not establish general accuracy, production readiness, hardware speedups or the quality of a fully trained model. Each chapter separates what its experiment demonstrates from what remains to be measured.

Course data fixtures and illustrations are original unless attributed. Linked papers, documentation and external datasets retain their respective licenses. Authoring notes, curriculum details and validation records live in the separate guidance directory.
