About First Principles

A course you can investigate.

This guide is for someone who knows everyday programming in Rust and wants to understand machine learning by building it.

The 56 chapters retain a path from one neuron through classical methods, neural networks, CPU and GPU computation, language models and advanced systems. Nine section projects connect the experiments. You begin with working programs, implement the important algorithms and test what changed.

Why these lessons use examples and experiments

Worked examples can reduce unnecessary search when an idea is new. Gradually removing guidance creates room to practice independently. Retrieval questions ask you to reconstruct an idea; predictions and changed inputs expose misunderstandings that a familiar successful run can hide. These choices draw on learning and programming-education research, with limits when transferred to independent adult study of machine learning in Rust.

The research and design report records primary sources, counterevidence, curriculum comparisons and the reasons for the design. The section map links the implementation organization; each section's research matrix connects objectives, explanations, practice and assessment.

What the checks can tell you

Numerical checks, unfamiliar inputs and complete solutions make the claimed behavior inspectable. They cannot establish understanding on their own. Browser illustrations do not execute the Rust files on your machine, and recorded Rust results are your own reports. Read, predict, implement, inspect and explain; use mistakes to choose the next experiment.

Small defaults and honest limits

Bundled datasets are tiny and course-authored unless their lesson says otherwise. They let you verify mechanics quickly, but do not establish real-world quality. Larger datasets, longer training, external services and GPU experiments require explicit choices. Hardware measurements identify the tested device and configuration. Conceptual diagrams and calculated operation counts are labeled as illustrations.

Your copy and your history

The guide is a static Python-generated website with local JavaScript interactions. Your current place and practice notes stay in browser storage. Download the source to run it locally and keep your code in Git. The redesigned exercises are separate from the original course's learner work and references.
