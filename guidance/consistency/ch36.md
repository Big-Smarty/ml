## Loss, gradient, shapes, and data

- `forward(&[usize])` maps token IDs `[T]` to row-major logits `[T,V]`. `loss(input, targets)` averages cross-entropy over the same `T` target rows. `loss_and_gradient(input, targets)` returns that identical mean loss plus derivatives of every parameter; it shares private forward caches and does not update the model.
- The caller continues to construct shifted next-token data explicitly: token window `[1,4,2,7]` becomes input `[1,4,2]` and targets `[4,2,7]`. Argument order, validation, stable log-sum-exp grouping, and numerical values are unchanged.
- `apply_sgd(&gradient, learning_rate)` remains one in-place optimizer update after the complete backward pass. The small demonstration now names `learning_rate` and uses `loss` for evaluation around fused gradient calls.
- The lesson now explains the ownership transition from the early scalar `step(data, learning_rate)`, which computes and applies its own gradient, to a separate `loss_and_gradient` plus `apply_sgd` boundary. This lets later optimizers reuse, accumulate, or selectively apply an already-computed gradient without changing what one optimizer step means.
- Chapter 36 retains contiguous row-major `[input features, output features]` projection weights so `A[m,k] × W[k,n]` can use Chapter 30 GEMM directly. The lesson now explains this as the storage transpose of earlier `[output,input]` dense weights and connects it to Chapter 33's `[D,V]` output projection. No checkpoint bytes were reordered.
