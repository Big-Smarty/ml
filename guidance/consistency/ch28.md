## Shapes, reduction, and Chapter 25 mapping

Both dot operands are contiguous `f32` slices of shape `[features]`; the result is one scalar sum over all paired elements. There is no average, loss, gradient, batch reduction, or parameter update. `dot_dispatch` and `dot_avx512_checked` reject unequal lengths. The internal scalar, `f64`, AVX2/FMA, and AVX-512 primitives receive lengths already established by a checked caller or closed fixture.

The lesson preserves Chapter 25's exact interface `dense(inputs, weights, bias, batch, in_features, out_features, outputs)` and shapes `inputs=[batch,in_features]`, `weights=[out_features,in_features]`, `bias=[out_features]`, and `outputs=[batch,out_features]`. One dense output is one input-row/weight-row dot product plus bias. Chapter 26's traversal and Chapter 27's row partitioning do not change that storage contract.

AVX2 consumes complete eight-value prefixes and AVX-512 consumes complete sixteen-value prefixes. Both pass the suffix to `dot_scalar`; empty, short, exact-width, and odd lengths retain complete coverage. The AVX2 path requires both runtime `avx2` and `fma` detection, while the explicit AVX-512 path requires `avx512f` detection. Architecture `cfg` gates retain scalar compilation paths outside x86/x86_64.
