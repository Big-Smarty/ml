# Chapter 03 consistency record

## Interface mapping

- Chapter 2's `Neuron { weight, bias }` becomes `LinearModel { weights, bias }` because Chapter 3 changes from one scalar input to multifeature linear regression. `input` becomes `features: [f64; 3]`; the one `weight` becomes three matching `weights`.
- `predict(self, features)` became `predict(&self, features)` and still returns one numerical prediction without changing the model.
- `loss(&self, data)` now provides a direct ordinary-MSE evaluation path on explicit data.
- `loss_and_gradient(self, data) -> Result<(f64, Self), _>` became `loss_and_gradient(&self, data) -> Result<(f64, Gradient), _>`. Derivatives no longer masquerade as a trained model.
- `gradient(&self, data)` exposes the derivatives of that same loss and reuses the fused calculation.
- The update previously embedded in `train` is now `step(self, data, learning_rate)`. `train(self, data, steps, learning_rate)` repeats that operation, and the public argument `rate` became `learning_rate`.
- `Scaler::fit` and `Scaler::transform` now use `data` and `features`; read-only transformation borrows `&self`.

The lesson's early continuity section links Chapter 2 and `/conventions.html`, states every retained responsibility and justified type/shape change, and includes the implemented operations and reductions. Its tagged derivative excerpt matches the reference source. The starter uses the same `LinearModel`, `Gradient`, prediction, loss, gradient, step, and train surface, leaving only `loss_and_gradient` as its guided TODO.

## Loss, gradient, and shape conventions

- `Features` is `[f64; 3]`; one `Example` is `(Features, f64)` in `(features, target)` order.
- `weights` and `Gradient::weights` each have three entries in the same documented order. `bias` and its derivative remain scalars.
- `predict` reduces the three corresponding weight-feature products by summation, then adds bias.
- `loss` is ordinary mean squared error over examples, with no half factor. `loss_and_gradient` reports the same mean reduction.
- Each weight derivative is the mean of `2 * (prediction - target) * feature`; the bias derivative is the mean of `2 * (prediction - target)`.
- `step` computes the complete `Gradient` at the unchanged model before updating any parameter. `train` counts optimizer updates as `steps`.
- Scaling fits one population mean and standard deviation per feature from training rows, then reuses those statistics for later features. The model continues to operate on the standardized `[f64; 3]` representation.
