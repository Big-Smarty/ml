# Independent review — chapters 01–19

Date: 2026-09-09
Reviewer: foundations audit agent
Scope: Sections 01–03, their nineteen lessons and metadata files, `labs/s01-foundations`, `labs/s02-neural-networks`, `labs/s03-tabular`, the preserved chapter 01–19 references used by those labs, and the learner/check/solution paths that support the written goals.

## Result

The redesigned chapters now form three coherent projects for an everyday Rust programmer who is new to machine learning:

1. Chapters 01–06 turn a runnable sensor rule into a trained linear predictor, a multifeature model, a probabilistic classifier, a grouped evaluation protocol, and a controlled optimization study.
2. Chapters 07–11 turn XOR's linear failure into a manually differentiated network, a scalar autodiff engine, batched dense kernels, a ten-class linear digit model, and a stateful MLP with momentum, Adam, diagnostics, and exact checkpoint continuation.
3. Chapters 12–19 use one disclosed maintenance table to study sampling uncertainty, train-only preprocessing, classical classifiers and ensembles, margins and kernels, PCA, clustering and density, then perform a frozen group/time model comparison with honest final error analysis.

Every ordinary chapter command starts from a bounded useful computation. Learners change central algorithm behavior or carry out a controlled experiment rather than fill an isolated expression. The goal checks call the learner implementations they claim to assess, and the completed implementations remain separate. All original `course.json` topics appear as substantive instruction in their original order. No open material defect remains in the reviewed scope after the resolutions below.

The heaviest conceptual jumps are Chapters 11, 15, 17, and 18. They are now divided into three or four 36–40 minute sessions, with concrete arithmetic, shapes, and failure cases before compact notation. The pacing is plausible as a plan, although actual learner completion time has not been measured.

## Prerequisite, source, and evidence judgment

The three projects accumulate state rather than restart nineteen miniature applications. Earlier ideas reappear when they are needed: simultaneous updates lead to vector gradients; logits lead to multiclass loss; manual chain-rule paths motivate autodiff; dense kernels support both digit models; grouped evaluation becomes grouped uncertainty and finally group/time cross-validation. The transition from close numerical guidance to experimental design is visible without fading into one-expression tasks.

Math is introduced just in time with actual inputs, intermediate arithmetic, dimensions, and reduction denominators. Chapters 01–06 require ordinary Rust arrays, loops, functions, borrowing, and `Result`, rather than prior calculus. Chapters 07–11 supply graph ownership, IDX parsing, checkpoint serialization, and CLI/file plumbing. Chapters 12–19 introduce statistics and classical ML from small counts and local vectors; Chapter 17 names the numerical-linear-algebra limitations rather than implying that its bounded power iteration is a general eigensolver.

The research records preserve the original sources, add primary papers or official technical references where needed, and distinguish source-supported definitions from course-authored datasets and measured fixture outputs. The lessons do not turn course usage, testimonials, or a cited teaching practice into evidence that this entire sequence is proven superior. Source-tagged preserved excerpts are identified as miniature references rather than results from the new cumulative labs.

## Remaining limits

Automated checks establish selected numerical behavior, error boundaries, and protocol invariants. They cannot grade a learner's explanation or prove that a numerically equivalent implementation used the intended derivation. Report additions such as a margin audit, eigenpair diagnostic, entropy comparison, shifted anomaly query, or final-day slice remain visible, self-assessed experiments with worked interpretation rather than brittle output-matching tests.

The datasets are controlled teaching fixtures. Section 02's default digit data is synthetic; real MNIST is an explicit local extension with no new performance claim. Section 03 has only six machines in its bootstrap/final groups and eighteen final rows, so its intervals and slices are deliberately fragile. Its kernel branch is a kernel perceptron, boosting uses squared residuals, PCA uses explicit covariance/power iteration, and the GMM is diagonal and locally optimized. These boundaries are stated in the lessons and metadata rather than hidden behind passing checks.

Chapter 19's final-data separation is an experimental protocol, not access control. The explicit final result is intentionally disappointing for the development-selected model. Keeping that result and refusing to switch to the final winner is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that the redesign teaches evaluation rather than score chasing.
